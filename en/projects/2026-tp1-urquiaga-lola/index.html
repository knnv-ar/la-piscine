Artwork presentation

Sin título

Artist: Lola Urquiaga
Year 2026
Semester First semester
Date September 17, 2026
License All rights reserved

This visual artwork is constructed through a repetition of lines connecting the left side of the screen to the right. At first, each line originates from a fixed point and extends in a straight, uniform manner, following a highly rigid set of rules. As we move downward through the composition, the tones gradually shift from deep black to a soft, light gray.

However, halfway through the composition, the rules change: the strict structure breaks apart, and the straight lines become curves. Through a fluid motion algorithm, these curves do not remain static, but move smoothly and unpredictably, as if they were ropes, threads, or pieces of fabric fluttering in the wind. The image transitions from a cold, geometric drawing into a living organism in constant transformation.

Technical, aesthetic, and conceptual development

The composition inhabits the threshold between structure and freedom. In its upper section, geometric order, symmetry, and calculated lines prevail. This represents structure, analytical thinking, and the rigidity of preconceived boundaries. It is the starting point, where everything is under control and each trajectory is predictable.

As the viewer’s gaze moves downward, the tone becomes lighter and the tension begins to dissolve. Straight geometry gives way to the looseness of curves, introducing an organic undulation that frees the lines from their immobility. What began as a disciplined scheme transforms into a living pulse, a dynamic flow in which structure surrenders to flexibility, demonstrating how the strict and systematic can naturally evolve toward the harmony of life and movement.

The transition from straight lines to curved and unpredictable forms reflects how technology can move from being a cold computational framework toward something full of life. In the upper section, the lines represent absolute control, order, and the precision of traditional computing. However, when the algorithm allows the lines to flow freely like fabric or ropes in the wind, the image ceases to be merely a geometric drawing produced by a computer and becomes a living organism.

It is precisely in this ability to break away from its own rigid rules that digital art finds its true magic. It demonstrates that technology is not only about calculating or repeating patterns, but also about creating movement, freedom, and continuous transformation.

Brief resume

My name is Lola Urquiaga, and I was born in the city of Buenos Aires on January 16, 1994. In my family, art and activism were part of everyday life. I grew up sleeping through rehearsals and backstage rooms, running between theater seats, and experimenting with different materials and surfaces in the workshop at home.

My creative journey has taken many forms, with dance, theater, and circus being my first loves. In 2014, I moved to the city of Mendoza to study for a Bachelor’s Degree in Visual Arts at the National University of Cuyo. During this period, I immersed myself in visual production and experimented with a wide range of techniques, disciplines, and materials.

Then, in one of those unexpected turns that life takes, I returned to the circus, this time working in different shows and circus tents. It was when I stepped away from the stage that my curiosity and thirst for discovering new forms of expression led me to explore the potential of multimedia art. Completely unaware of where to begin, I discovered the Multimedia Arts degree at the National University of the Arts (UNA).

Today, I am exploring this new form of expression, learning how to translate the lines and images in my mind into code and bring them into existence within this world of infinite possibilities.
