Press the E key to generate Entropy

Recommended to read previously: https://blog.cloudflare.com/es-es/harnessing-office-chaos/

Technical, aesthetic and conceptual development

This piece of generative art (1) is expressly a nod to Cloudflare’s Entropy Wall**(2)**, which is located at its San Francisco headquarters, visible from a stained glass window overlooking the street.

I think it is important to clarify that prior to Cloudflare, this idea was patented by Silicon Graphics as Lavarand to increase protection in the browser’s SSL encryption. Netscape in 1996, since it used a vulnerable and predictable algorithm. Cloudflare launched its instantiation of a Lavarand system in 2017.

Quoted from Wikipedia (3): “A proprietary SGI O2 camera with a 512 × 480 pixel CCD (Charge Coupled Device) sensor for image capture; and an array of six Lava Lite lamps as a chaotic source(…)” “Lavarand was designed to directly generate a continuous stream of cryptographically sound random numbers.(…)""

Today, with many changes, improvements and an expired patent, Cloudflare continues to apply this method to generate “cryptographically sound” hashes for the same protocol (SSL and TLS) My impulses to insist on the superiority of the tangible led me to this whole search and even to produce my last practical work on this subject around what Approximately 10% of the Internet and proxy security of many of us runs and depends, at some point, on lava lamps. Same idea (improved) persisting 30 years ago. Inke Arns in “The code as a performative speech act” (2005) (1) presents this same concept of title and serves to deepen this reflection.

“To John Langshaw Austin (How to Do Things with Words, Harvard University, 1955) Linguistic expressions not only serve the purpose of describing a factual situation, but They are also used to execute or perform acts. The theory of acts Austin’s speech theory considers speech fundamentally as action, and its effectiveness is not measured by the results that thanks to it are obtained, but by speech itself. Here it is precisely where the theory of speech acts is related to the assumption ta performativity of the code: “[when] a word not only means something, but performatively generates exactly what its meaning designates.”

“This «coded performativity» has con- political and immediate sequences in real and virtual spaces (among others, on the Internet), in which we move and live more and more: that means, ultimately, that this per- Codified formativity mobilizes or immobilizes its users. Of This way the code becomes law”

The search to represent this with an individual lamp synthesizes (and also optimizes) the importance of the creative proposal, even in technical and exact environments. From the “Multimedia” context, today more than ever I believe that exercising this same thing is extremely urgent. This utopian idea that the Software is going to take everything ahead, and it will be a self-supporting infrastructure and much less necessary for humans to understand. If there is something fascinating about the phrase “The Internet runs in lava lamps” it is that something tangible and aesthetically impossible to associate with computing is related. These types of anecdotes are what motivate me to research and produce and I think many people agree with me. In a previous section Arns exposes a series of works and automatically connected my idea with the last one exhibited called “Sealed Computers” by Maurizio Bolognini (since 1992).

“This consists of a total of more than 200 computers, which produce and exchange bian a constant stream of random images. These ordered- are sealed and left running indefinitely, disconnecting ted from any type of external device (for example, a monitor). Since there is no visible result of what these machines generated and exchanged, we are left with a certain feeling uncomfortable to be faced with an independent, invisible performance, impossible to control.”

Taking this idea from Bolognini, of generating discomfort through the absence of proper hardware functioning reminds us of the fundamental What is it for us that without an “output” we are left out of the intervention? of systems that manage information. My final reflection is to understand that, both on a technical and artistic level, a human, physical and tangible “input” will always be necessary. When languages, applications, all kinds of of multimedia content and a long etcetera in a matter of seconds and these themselves are ephemeral, that no technology can match the randomness of a physical object, reminds me that There are still infinite things to create and design, that all this demotivation to produce that stalks us when large language models enter the conversation, is a product of this utopia that they are selling us, of our ignorance about the real limitations of software and our (imposed) obsession with efficiency, productivity and computing capacity, when there are great ideas that can work even on the oldest devices.

Brief Biography

Juan Luciano Derene is currently pursuing a degree in Multimedia Arts at the Department of Multimedia and Electronic Arts of the National University of the Arts; he also holds a diploma in Visual Arts. His work aims to sublimate both collective and personal needs, prioritizing reflection and social critique. The relationship with technology—including its obsolescence and the precarious nature of technological systems—is a prominent feature and a central axis of his creative practice. For Juan, the sociocultural context in which we grow up reveals far more about us than is typically acknowledged.
