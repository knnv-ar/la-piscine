Artwork presentation

genesis-luz-mala

Artist: santiago valentin stillitano
Year 2026
Semester First semester
Date July 8, 2026
License All rights reserved

brief description of the work

this piece takes as its starting point a work by lillian schwartz, :: night scene (1975) ::. the piece depicts an urban landscape that is reconfigured through the intersection and meeting of lines printed on the digital canvas. the visual composition is based on the geometric tension between the static and rigid (of the linear grids) against the seemingly endless oscillations of modules and fragments that simulate buildings. the development was a collage process that lasted almost the entire winter break, involving modification, subtraction, and pixel-by-pixel assembly based on the image I have in my head of the Dock Sud neighborhood and its monoblocks. I try to articulate the representation of a map with the tension of living and interacting in contemporary common spaces.

technical, aesthetic, and conceptual development

I use software as a tool to explore the resources that generative processes offer in illustration. with high contrast, I aim to highlight the manipulation and randomness of opacity, generating a simulation of bioluminescent depths through algorithmic calculation; so that light becomes the result of interactions, variations, and transparencies as in everyday life // day_to_day // night_to_night //, avoiding a smooth white color on a virtual canvas.

in this territory, the visual composition holds a confrontation between the order of the perpendicular and the interruption of the anomaly’s engine that trembles through random but pristine mishaps; while the repetition of crossing lines and pieces presents the urban sensation of the dense and hard rigidity, the trembling circles function as that soft element that seeks and, although gentle, breaks with the flat geometry through a fictional and algorithmic intelligence.

contemporary art, according to Brea, is an art of significant production in relation to subjects of experience, who operate within the event and presence; subjects who do not preexist affectively to the production of the particular work but to the effects generated by its circulation, the work itself, and its conditions. the precarious individuation of the artist and their immaterial and symbolic work directly addresses the supposed disconnection between the spheres of the real world and the other // ”(…) there is no such thing as this world and the other; art can no longer claim to inhabit an autonomous sphere, a separate domain.” (Brea, 2008, p. 107). // the landscape is presented as one that seeks communication and friction, that seeks intellectual coexistence and interaction between subjectivities that brush against and discomfort each other.

at the same time, the presence of light fanning out from a focal point acts by cutting the scene with aggressive diagonals. that bad light projects one’s own artistic practice: an oblique search that flashes and bursts into fragments in the lives of others, introducing interference and noise into the program.

as Brea points out in the Antiglosario ;; the true tool of art is not the computer itself, but the immanent self-criticism:: that exploration of the very limits of generative language. I do not intend for this scene to have pure signals or clean perspectives; the interference that runs through the work along with the matter of the night that shines through the monitor reveal that the machinery, the city, and the council are neither static nor silent but are distorted by the necessity of our immaterial work to produce meaning.

bibliography

BREA, José Luis. (2002). “Brief (and disordered)” anti-glossary - or dictionary of topics - on electronic art. BREA, José Luis. (2008). “Redefinition of Artistic Practices”

Artist Biography:

santiago stillitano, born in 2005, studied for 1 year in the Bachelor’s degree in Arts at the Faculty of Philosophy and Letters and in search of more practical content, started the Multimedia Arts program at UNA in 2025.
