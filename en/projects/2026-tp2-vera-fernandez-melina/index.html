Artwork presentation

recuerdos de verano

Artist: melina vera fernandez
Year 2026
Semester Second semester
Date November 25, 2026
License CC BY-SA 4.0

Brief Description of the Artwork My work emerges from the need to hold on to nostalgia, both human and technological. Through blurred faces and outdated web formats, I seek to recover what was once thought to be lost. It is an interactive piece: with every click, we unlock a brief memory, and if we give ourselves a space, we can musically pause the flow of memory.

Technical, aesthetic, and conceptual development

Working on this piece allowed me to revisit my blog from when I was twelve years old, my fascination with customizing HTML code, filling the screen with GIFs, choosing a banner or background, and, of course, personalizing it with music. Drawing inspiration from the aesthetics of websites such as Tumblr, Blogspot, MySpace, and others, which were built around each user’s interests, I sought to recreate that experience. I knew that simply imitating the aesthetic would be pointless; it was also necessary to bring back devices that have become obsolete in the modern world we live in today.

So I went through my drawers and came across an old digital camera: a 2003 Concord 3040 Eye-Q AF. It has only 3.1 megapixels and an SD card containing videos I recorded during a summer in the past. I became even more fascinated when I discovered that the brand ceased production in 2009. It made me realize that I was holding a device someone had discarded because its image quality could no longer compete with today’s cameras. Someone who had decided to sell it to me years ago for an amount that today would not even reach five thousand pesos. Yet, to me, it was worth so much more than that, because even today, it continues to live on through me.

Working on this project also led me to reflect on the first text I read for this course, A Brief (and Disordered) Anti-Glossary on Electronic Art by José Luis Brea. The text discusses the evolution of technologies and how their progress seems to devour everything in its path. It also mentions different technological art mediums and how, nowadays, they have “died.” Something similar happens here: in the previous paragraph, I find myself mentioning websites that have been forgotten or are no longer popular. Very few people still choose to maintain, for example, a Blogspot. It has been replaced by much “cooler” writing platforms such as Medium or Substack. Being a blogger no longer means personalizing your space, because now, in order to access those personal features, you have to pay for premium functions.

My progress throughout the course has allowed me to question how I use code. After reading Inke Arns, who argues that software can be an artistic material with a cultural impact, I realized that I am not using it in a neutral way. On the contrary, I am consciously thinking about it, and its functions serve a purpose in my work. Other works and the narratives they explore also helped me throughout this process, such as insert_coin and its approach to the role of the user. It encouraged me to reflect on our passivity in the face of virtual platforms. We often fail to question changes, even when they make our everyday interactions less comfortable. Personally, I enjoy having the ability to intervene, and I would like to incorporate more user interactions into my future works.

Finally, another text that greatly influenced my perspective throughout the course was the one by César Aira. It helped me think about the notions of what is considered art and what is not, as well as the possibility of presenting something and not being liked. If I were trying to make “good art,” I would not have chosen that camera. Instead, I would have focused on its resolution, on showcasing an expensive product and the craftsmanship behind it. I would have followed the already established standards and ended up producing what the author defines as “craftsmanship.”

Bibliography

  • Aira, C. (2013). On Contemporary Art. Grijalbo-Mondadori.
  • Brea, J. L. (2002). A Brief and Disordered Anti-Glossary or Dictionary of Clichés on Electronic Art.
  • Arns, Inke. (2005). “Code as a Performative Speech Act.” Artnodes Journal, July 2005, ISSN 1695-5951.

Brief resume

My name is Melina Vera, I am 25 years old, and this is my first year studying Multimedia Arts at UNA (National University of the Arts). After completing two years of Audiovisual Arts, I decided to change degree programs in order to expand my ideas and creative possibilities. I enjoy taking photographs and recording videos. Through this degree, I hope to learn new tools that will allow my little self to bring her ideas to life.
